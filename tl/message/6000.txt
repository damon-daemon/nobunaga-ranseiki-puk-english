17th head of the Tozawa. Lord of Kakunodate Castle. Eldest son of Michimori. Chronically ill, he passed the headship early to his brother Moriyasu and took vows. Later returned to lay life as Masashige's adopted son.
@6420
18th head of the Tozawa. Lord of Kakunodate Castle. 3rd son of Michimori. Bold campaigns built the Tozawa's largest domain. Nicknamed "Yasha Kuro", yet said to have been merciful too.
@6422
20th head of the Tozawa. Lord of Kakunodate Castle. Son of Moriyasu by a concubine. Inherited on uncle Mitsumori's death. Took the Uesugi's Sakata Castle at Sekigahara; guarded Odawara Castle at Osaka.
@6424
Tozawa vassal. 3rd son of 14th head Yukimori. Styled Awa no kami, lived at Kodate. Later adopted Moroshige (eldest son of nephew Michimori), who had taken Buddhist vows.
@6426
Tozawa vassal. Said to be of Nanbu origin. Fought well with his lord Mitsumori in the Kunohe revolt. Noticed by Hideyoshi, went to Osaka but returned to the Tozawa after the Toyotomi Hidetsugu affair.
@6428
Tozawa vassal. Lord of Dewa Tomaki Castle. At Sekigahara marched with Mogami Yoshiaki of the Eastern Army and fought Ono-dera Yoshimichi's Western force, falling at the Kakumagawa.
@6430
12th head of the Onodera. Eldest son of Yasumichi. Went to Kyoto to serve the shogunate, but returned and inherited on his father's death. Later caught up in a power struggle and killed by his vassals.
@6432
13th head of the Onodera. Eldest son of Koremichi. After his father's death sheltered by the Daihoji, then killed enemies including Yokote Mitsumori and returned to Yokote Castle, bringing the Onodera to their peak.
@6434
14th head of the Onodera. 2nd son of Kagemichi. Joined Hideyoshi's Odawara campaign and had his lands confirmed. Ignored Tokugawa's call to march at Sekigahara and was dispossessed after the war.
@6436
Onodera vassal. Born a Sasaki. Moved from Omi to Ogachi district, Dewa, served the Onodera and received Sakenobe. Lost a battle to the Daihoji and died soon after.
@6438
Onodera vassal. Lord of Sakenobe Castle. Son of Sadatsuna. Lost to Mogami Yoshiaki and submitted. Won merit quelling an uprising in Senboku and fighting Uesugi forces that invaded Mogami land.
@6440
Onodera vassal. Lord of Yokote Castle. In 1546 rose with Kanazawa Kinjobo and killed his lord Koremichi. Later killed by Onodera Terumichi, Koremichi's eldest son.
@6442
Dewa gentry. Lord of Hondo Castle. Son of Tomochika. Joined Hideyoshi's Odawara campaign and had his lands confirmed. In the Korean campaign served under Maeda Toshinaga at Nagoya Castle, Hizen.
@6444
Dewa gentry. Joined Hideyoshi's Odawara campaign and had his lands confirmed. Guarded Nagoya Castle, Hizen, in the Korean campaign. Fought at Sekigahara and Osaka, later holding Dewa Honjo (20,000 koku).
@6446
Dewa gentry. Son of Sumiuji. The Daihoji began when founder Muto Ujihira became steward of Oizumi estate, Shonai, in the Kamakura period. The Daihoji name dates from 6th head Nagamori.
@6448
Dewa gentry. Son of Kuro (Masauji's brother). Fought the Sunagoe (Daihoji cadet) often; in 1532 his seat Tsurugaoka Castle was burned and he moved his base to Oura Castle.
@6450
Dewa gentry. Lord of Oura Castle. Constant warfare and neglect of government made his people hate him as the "wicked lord". Killed himself after vassal Maemori Kurando, allied with Mogami Yoshiaki, rebelled.
@6452
Dewa gentry. Lord of Oura Castle. Inherited on his brother Yoshiuji's death. Adopted Uesugi vassal Honjo Shigenaga's 2nd son to tie the Uesugi closer, but Mogami Yoshiaki attacked and he killed himself.
@6454
2nd son of Honjo Shigenaga. Adopted by Daihoji Yoshioki and took the Daihoji with Uesugi Kagekatsu's backing. Exiled to Yamato for suspected incitement of an uprising, pardoned, served the Uesugi.
@6456
Daihoji vassal. First called Maemori Kurando. Allied with Mogami Yoshiaki, rebelled and killed his lord Yoshiuji. Governed Shonai on Yoshiaki's orders, but fought Honjo Shigenaga and died.
@6458
Daihoji vassal. Brother of Yoshinaga. After lord Yoshiuji's death made keeper of Oura Castle by his brother. Lost to Honjo Shigenaga at Jugoriga-hara, then failed to assassinate him and was killed.
@6460
Daihoji vassal. The Tosarin held the Haguro-san headship, later seized by the Daihoji. Zento grew close to the Uesugi, clashing with his lord, and was destroyed.
@6462
One of the Yuri Twelve Bands. The Nikaho began when Tomotaka, son of Oi Tomomitsu, moved from Shinano to Nikaho, Dewa. In the Sengoku era it became the Twelve Bands' central house.
@6464
Head of the Yuri Twelve Bands. Garrisoned Nagoya Castle, Hizen, in the Bunroku campaign. Praised by Tokugawa Ieyasu for the Aizu campaign. Joined the East at Sekigahara; confirmed at 5,000 koku.
@6466
10th head of the Mogami. Inherited at 2. Doted on his 2nd son Yoshitoki and tried to pass the headship to him, but eldest son Yoshiaki rose during the Date disturbance and forced his retirement.
@6468
11th head of the Mogami. Eldest son of Yoshimori. Forced his father to retire and killed his brother to become head. By secret diplomacy and assassinating enemy elders, built the Mogami's largest domain.
@6470
12th head of the Mogami. 2nd son of Yoshiaki. Succeeded his father. Killed rival brother Yoshichika and worked to unify the domain, but died suddenly, said poisoned by uncle Tateoka Mitsunao.
@6472
Mogami vassal. Lord of Shimizu Castle. Adopted Yoshichika, 3rd son of his lord Yoshiaki. Repelled repeated Daihoji attacks with Yoshiaki's help. His daughter became Yoshiaki's concubine.
@6474
3rd son of Mogami Yoshiaki. Said to be born the same year as his brother Ieshika. Adopted by Shimizu Yoshiuji. Fell out with his brother and killed himself when attacked by Ieshika's men.
@6476
4th son of Mogami Yoshiaki. After brother Ieshika died, backed over nephew Yoshitoshi as head, touching off the Mogami succession dispute and the house's dispossession. Later an elder of Mito Tokugawa.
@6478
Mogami vassal. In the Tenbun War went out as deputy for his lord Yoshimori. In the succession dispute, though gravely ill, persuaded Yoshimori to pass the headship to Yoshiaki.
@6480
Mogami vassal. Son of Sadanao. Skilled in strategy, he contributed greatly to the defeat of the Tendo and Shiratori. Also served as envoy to the Date and took Mamuro Castle, active on all fronts.
@6482
Mogami vassal. From the Narisawa house, succeeded Morimune. Fought hard against Uesugi Kagekatsu's invading army at Sekigahara. When Ieshika became head, took charge of governing the domain.
@6484
Mogami vassal. Styled Daigaku. Ended the water shortage on the Shonai plain with flood works. The weir was called "Daigaku Weir"; his lord Yoshiaki hailed it as "Shonai's treasure for ages to come."
@6486
Mogami vassal. Close aide of Yoshiaki, of whom it was said "no foe, however strong, did not yield to him." At Sekigahara held Hasedo Castle and repelled Naoe Kanetsugu's great army.
@6488
Mogami vassal. Lord of Yuzawa Castle. At Sekigahara, attacked by the Onodera, he fought alone and held the castle. His fief of 48,000 koku was the largest among Mogami vassals.
@6490
Mogami vassal. Styled Inaba no kami. Began a canal weir to draw Akagawa water, but it was halted when the house was dispossessed and he was placed in Doi Toshikatsu's custody. Finished later as "Inaba Weir."
@6492
Mogami vassal. First served the Tendo, then made peace on condition his eldest son Yasumitsu wed Yoshiaki's daughter Matsuo. A man of great strength and valour; Yoshiaki secretly feared him.
@6494
Mogami vassal. When Date Harumune and Terumune fell out, went to the Date as envoy urging reconciliation. At the Mogami-Date reconciliation meeting, acted as deputy for his lord Yoshiaki.
@6496
14th head of the Date. Won the Oshu Commissioner post and expanded his domain by marriage diplomacy. Clashed with heir Harumune over placing 3rd son Sanemoto in the Uesugi, causing the Tenbun War.
@6498
15th head of the Date. Eldest son of Tanemune. Fought his father in the Tenbun War, then took the headship once it ended. Like his father used marriage diplomacy to secure the Date's standing.
